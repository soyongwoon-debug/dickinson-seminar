## Slide 2 — Two Epigraphs: The Central Tension

> "Why not an 'eleventh hour' in the life of the **mind** as well as such an one in the life of the **soul**..."
> — Dickinson to Austin, 1851 (L44)

> "the more consciousness, the more intense the despair."
> — Kierkegaard, *Sickness unto Death*

- Dickinson: thinking is an existential drama, equal to the soul's.
- Kierkegaard: deeper consciousness = deeper despair.
- **The paper lives in the gap between these two claims.**

## Slide 3 — The Critical Debate

**The received view (what Deppman opposes):**
- Dickinson privileges emotional / physical response over clarity of thought.
- Margaret Peterson: her impassioned poems become *"a series of ecstatic assertions, an abandonment to excess verging on mental unbalance"* (500).

**Deppman's entry point:**
- Her poems' "famous opacity" has led readers to treat affect as primary.
- He will "argue the opposite case."

*Speaker note (2 min):* Set up the boxing match — mystic vs. thinker.

---

## Slide 4 — The Thesis

> "in what follows I will argue the opposite case and portray her **not as a mystic but as a serious thinker**." (p. 85)

- Not "ecstatic assertions" → but *"careful sequences of ideas and images."*
- Not "abandonment to excess" → but *"thoughtful production of, and reaction to, extreme states of being."*

**Stated goal:** how Dickinson conceived *the activity of thinking*, how she linked thought and poetry, and *"how writing poetry helped her think."* (p. 85)

*Speaker note (1.5 min):* This is THE sentence. Read it aloud verbatim.

## Slide 6 — Evidence ②: The Many Faces of Thought

Thought is a **"consistent as well as kaleidoscopic"** topic (p. 85):

- **Celebratory:** *"Best Things dwell out of Sight / ... Our Thought"* (Fr1012)
- **Cautionary:** *"If wrecked upon the Shoal of Thought..."* (Fr1503)
- **Analytic (size/power):** *"The Brain - is wider than the Sky -"* (Fr598)
- **Wild / dangerous:** *"The Brain, within it's Groove / Runs evenly ... But let a Splinter swerve -"* (Fr563)
- **Sufficient for happiness:** *"The revery alone will do, / If bees are few"* (Fr1779)

*Speaker note (1.5 min):* Dickinson theorizes thought from many angles — it's a deliberate subject, not a byproduct.

## Slide 7 — Framework (1): Postmodern Dickinson

Deppman places her on a critical-theory map:

- **Wolff:** "artist of the age of transition" — freeing language "from the tyranny of His [God's] definitions" (429).
- **Porter:** the mind *"explosive with signifying power but disinherited from transcendent knowledge"* (7).
- **Lyotard:** postmodernism = *"incredulity toward metanarratives"* — she neither accepts nor rejects her era's discourses.
- **Derrida:** Dickinson as *"bricoleuse,"* mixing religious/literary/scientific vocabularies.

**Root condition:** the tension between **Lockean empiricism** (her schoolbooks) and **Kantian / Transcendentalist** apprehension of the supersensible (p. 86).

*Speaker note (2 min):* She is a "postmodern artist avant la lettre."

---

## Slide 8 — Framework (2): The Kantian Sublime

**The engine of the whole paper.**

The Kantian scene (which Lyotard turns into "the postmodern condition"):

1. Reason conceives something **conceivable but unpresentable** (infinity, death, the very large/small).
2. Reason demands an adequate **image**.
3. Imagination **tries — and fails.**
4. The mind repeats the try; from this the **sublime** emerges.

> Lyotard: *"it is our business not to supply reality but to invent allusions to the conceivable which cannot be presented"* (Postmodern 81).

**⚠️ Key concession:** Dickinson *"did not read Kant"* (p. 87) — Deppman claims a *shared attitude*, not influence.

*Speaker note (2.5 min):* Draw the try→fail→repeat loop on the slide. Highlight the "did not read Kant" concession — we'll come back to it in the critique.

## Slide 9 — The Origin of "Trying" (L10, 1846)

At age 15, Dickinson tries to think her own death:

> "I cannot imagine with the farthest stretch of my imagination my own death scene... I cannot realize that the grave will be my last home..."

- The refrain: *"I cannot imagine ... I cannot realize ... I cannot realize ... nor can I realize ..."*
- The mind *"stretches, fails, realizes it fails, regroups, rewords, and reaches its limit again"* (p. 88).
- **The core formula:** *"she cannot think death or Eternity ... but she cannot not think them either."*

*Speaker note (1.5 min):* This IS the title. "Trying to think" = thinking what cannot be thought, yet cannot be avoided.

## Slide 10 — Evidence ③: The Higginson Letters

Dickinson's only professional correspondence — writing understood **as thought**.

- **1862 (L260):** *"Are you too deeply occupied to say if my Verse is alive?"* — she asks about the *life of the thought*, not form.
- **The epistemological bind (L260):** *"The Mind is so near itself – it cannot see, distinctly – and I have none to ask –."*
- **Writing = psychotherapeutic thought (L261):** *"I had a terror ... and so I sing, as the Boy does by the Burying Ground – because I am afraid."*
- **Thought over language (L261):** *"While my thought is undressed – I can make the distinction, but when I put them in the Gown – they look alike, and numb."*
- **To the end (L1042):** *"bereft of Book and Thought, by the doctor's reproof."*

*Speaker note (2 min):* Note the frustration when Higginson replies about form ("gait spasmodic") — she wanted judgment of her *thinking*.

## Slide 11 — Defining the "Try-to-Think" Poem

Deppman's central concept:

- Purpose: **to force the mind to do something extremely difficult** — to satisfy reason's unsatisfiable demand for a complete image.
- Features: *"the try is usually serious, the goal explicitly stated, ... emphasis squarely on the willful movements of thought."*
- They are *"precisely sequenced, if difficult, thought experiments"* — readers are invited to **repeat the steps.**

**Examples:** "Of Death I try to think like this" (Fr1588) · "The nearest Dream recedes" (Fr304B) · "I think To Live - may be a Bliss" (Fr757, a "try-to-*believe*" poem).

*Speaker note (1.5 min):* This genre-concept is the paper's original contribution.

## Slide 12 — Close Reading (1): "I tried to think a lonelier Thing" (Fr570, 1863)

**[Display the full poem]**

> I tried to think a lonelier Thing / Than any I had seen -
> Some Polar Expiation - An Omen in the Bone
> Of Death's tremendous nearness -
> I probed Retrieveless things / My Duplicate - to borrow -
> A Haggard comfort springs / From the belief that Somewhere -
> Within the Clutch of Thought - / There dwells one other Creature
> Of Heavenly Love - forgot -
> I plucked at our Partition - / As One should pry the Walls -
> Between Himself - and Horror's Twin - / Within Opposing Cells -
> I almost strove to clasp his Hand, / Such Luxury - it grew -
> That as Myself - could pity Him - / Perhaps he - pitied me -

**The interpretive dilemma (p. 94):**
- Proactive? — a *"virtuoso attempt to conceptualize an extreme human possibility"*
- Reactive? — an attempt *"to knead it, battle it, alter it, realize it, or just survive it through thought"*
- **Deppman chooses: reactive.**

*Speaker note (2 min):* Read the poem slowly. Note the nervous vocabulary — "not a family of overconfident words."

---

## Slide 13 — Close Reading (2): Tracking the Steps

The mind's sequence, step by step:

1. **Naming the "Thing"** → two "quiddities":
   - *"Some Polar Expiation"* — expiation + *polar expedition*: radical exile from self, culture, nature.
   - *"An Omen in the Bone / Of Death's tremendous nearness"* — anticipatory; death near in time AND space.
2. **Borrowing a "Duplicate"** → a fellow spirit *"Of Heavenly Love - forgot -"*: the *"smallest possible unit of imagined community"* (pp. 97–98).
3. **"Within the Clutch of Thought"** — decisive **undecidability**: reachable *by* thought, or made *only of* thought (= fabricated)? (p. 98)
4. **"our Partition"** — the possessive forms *"a we"*: bridge AND barrier between the living self and the dead twin.

> Autobiographical support (L39, 1850): *"when the unreconciled spirit has nothing left but God, that spirit is lone indeed."*

*Speaker note (2.5 min):* This is the heart of the talk — show how the theory (Slide 8) actually operates on the text.

## Slide 14 — Close Reading (3): The Paradoxical Close

> I almost strove to clasp his Hand, / Such Luxury - it grew -
> That as Myself - could pity Him - / Perhaps he - pitied me -

- Two God-forsaken souls in "Opposing Cells" — Deppman: *"a chilling scene reminiscent of Beckett's Godot."*
- Deppman hears an **elided phrase**: *"Such Luxury - it grew [- to think] / That as Myself - could pity Him -."*
- → Consolation comes from **the experimental force of thought itself.**
- **But:** *"a fragile state and a momentary victory"* — how long can meditating on one's dead duplicate console?

*Speaker note (1.5 min):* The poem ends in arrested motion — a "stop-motion picture," no dialectical resolution.

## Slide 15 — Critical Assessment: Strengths

**What the paper achieves:**

1. **Methodological rigor** — grounds a large claim in concordance data (p. 86), not mere impression. A falsifiable, checkable move.
2. **Genuine integration** — letters (L10, L260, L261) function as *evidence of a poetics*, not decorative biography.
3. **A generative concept** — the "try-to-think poem" names a real, repeatable pattern and can be applied to poems beyond Deppman's sample.
4. **Reframes opacity as method** — difficulty is not failure or excess; it is the *trace* of a mind at work.

*Speaker note (1.5 min):* Give the paper its due before pushing back.

---

## Slide 16 — Critical Assessment: My Position

**Claim: the thesis is self-undermining — and that is its most interesting result.**

- Deppman sets up **thought vs. feeling** and picks thought. Yet his own reading of Fr570 runs on **affect**: *"desperate," "chilling," "sickening loneliness,"* and writing as *"thought's psychotherapeutic response to troubling emotion"* (p. 90).
- If the poem's "try" only matters *because* the loneliness is unbearable, then **thought and feeling are inseparable** — the binary collapses.
- **So the paper's real achievement is the opposite of its stated one:** not "thought over feeling," but a demonstration that Dickinson's thinking *is* an emotional act.

**Secondary pressure points (discussion bait):**
- **"Kant without Kant"** (p. 87): historical claim or the critic's imported lens?
- **Circularity:** the "try-to-think poem" is defined *from* the poems it then explains.
- **Live dispute:** Deppman rejects Muldoon's Civil War reading of Fr570 (note 18) — is his refusal justified?

*Speaker note (2 min):* Commit to the stance. Invite disagreement explicitly.

## Slide 17 — Conclusion & Discussion Questions

**Deppman's ending:** we never know if she *succeeded*; these are poems in which she *"tried to help or save herself by representing her own efforts to help or save herself"* (p. 100).

- To join the poem is to risk *"experiencing a loneliness we cannot sound."*
- **Return to Kierkegaard:** the more consciousness, the more despair. Thought is both consolation AND risk.

**Discussion questions:**
1. Does the thought/feeling binary survive Deppman's own close reading? Should it?
2. Is the Kantian sublime a valid frame for a poet who never read Kant?
3. Is "try-to-think" a discovered genre or a critic's construct?
4. Whose reading of Fr570 do you find more convincing — Deppman's or Muldoon's?

*Speaker note (1.5 min):* End on the questions, not a summary. Hand the floor to the room.
